Daikatana's game code, in FTE.

From the Daikatana restoration project (dlls/physics, dlls/world, dlls/weapons, user = IonCommon; the headers
they take from the engine in include/). The game was three DLLs; here they're one: physics' loader
(physics/dk_static.cpp) calls world's and weapons' dll_Entry itself and looks their exports up in
fte/dk_exports.cpp. What would meet another module's names, or FTE's, is renamed (include/dk_rename_*.h): what
more than one DLL defined, in physics and weapons; everything with C linkage and every global variable (g++
doesn't mangle those), in all of them. Both are generated by mkstatic.py from the objects' symbols (nm -A): run
it after adding code that defines or uses something new across modules, and build again.

fte/ is what puts it on FTE's Quake II server: svdk_game.cpp gives FTE a game API 3 export table over physics'
interface (shadow edicts, as server/svq2_rerelease.c does for the Quake II rerelease's game), dk_pmove.cpp is
Daikatana's engine's player movement. server/svdk_fte.c is FTE's side of it. Daikatana (-daikatana, the
FTE-Daikatana game) runs it instead of looking for a game library. cin_*.cpp are its engine's cinematics' playback
(dk_gce_main.cpp, dk_cin_playback.cpp, dk_gce_load.cpp, without its editor), here beside the game: the scripts it
starts play their entities through the world's interface, their camera is the world's cinematic camera, which
the player's view follows (its server sent the view entity's place as the player's).

client/ is its client's code on FTE's client (the dkclient library, sources_client.txt; its client's headers in
client/dkinc): the HUD, its own messages (svcq2dk_message, fte/dk_fteproto.h), temp events and effects, and its
entities: FTE's client keeps Quake II's entity state (which carries its renderfx, effects, frames as they are) and
hands it over each frame (client/cldk_fte.c), what Quake II's has no room for (frameInfo, effects2, render_scale,
modelindex2..4...) comes reliably as it changes (svcdk_fte_entstate), and cl_ents.cpp draws and animates them as
its client did (cldk_ents.cpp builds its frame). cldk_fte.h is all it has of FTE.

Also its client's: menu/ (its menus, as FTE's menu while it's the game), its crosshair, scoreboard
(dk_scoreboard.cpp), fog (FTE's linear fog: the gamemode sets r_fog_linear), skins (an image on every surface of
a dkm but its SRF_STATICSKIN ones, its players' from their CS_PLAYERSKINS strings), looping sounds' volumes and
distances, and player movement for prediction (cldk_pred.cpp). With no default.cfg, its daikatana.cfg is the
default config (its binds and settings, less its engine's video, sound and network drivers'), and its userinfo
cvars (modelname, character...) are FTE's.

Building (CMakeLists.txt, FTE_GAME_DAIKATANA, the dkgame library): sources.txt lists what's compiled, each with
-include include/dk_fte_compat.h (what MSVC and its CRT had, elsewhere), -DDK_MODULE_<directory> (physics,
world, weapons, user, fte), -DTONGUE_ENGLISH -DC_ONLY, the directories user, include, physics, world, weapons, fte
and the source's own on the include path before FTE's. C++ (gnu++11, -fpermissive: it was written for MSVC 6).

Changes from the original are marked FTE where they aren't mechanical: const CVector& for what MSVC let
temporaries bind to, MSVC 6's for-scope (loop variables hoisted), Windows-only bits.
